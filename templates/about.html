About

Countries

Three countries today: India, the United States and Germany. Any other country is never approved automatically: it goes to a reviewer.

  • India: GSTIN (with check character) and PAN, IFSC.
  • United States: EIN, 9-digit routing number (with checksum).
  • Germany: VAT ID (DE plus 9 digits, with check digit), IBAN (with mod-97 checksum and the 22-character length) and BIC. A German company banking abroad is allowed but goes to a reviewer. Umlauts match the way banks spell them (Müller = Mueller).

More countries are a rule-table change, not a rewrite: each identifier format is one row in the FORMATS table in checks.py, and the IBAN rule is shared by every IBAN country, so the UK, France or Italy would mostly be new VAT-number rows. We deliberately did not try all ten of the biggest countries: every country that is added without being tested properly is a place where the process could wrongly approve someone. Names that are not in Latin letters (for example Japanese or Chinese) are never compared by spelling: they go to a reviewer.

Who uses this

  • Vendors fill in the form and upload documents. They see only the status and what they need to fix.
  • Reviewers use the Reviewer desk: a queue that can be sorted by age and searched, with the evidence and documents, a way to take a submission so nobody else works on it, and Approve / Reject / Request more info with a required reason. After each decision the next submission opens.

When is a submission rejected?

  • Two or more contradictions at once (for example GSTIN and PAN disagree and the PAN type does not fit): a typo does not explain that. One contradiction alone is a mistake the vendor can fix.
  • A duplicate with nothing new: a company that is already onboarded applies again with exactly the same details.
  • The name is on the sanctions watchlist (an exact match). A name that is only close goes to a reviewer, because false matches are common.
  • The bank is in an embargoed country (Iran, North Korea, Syria, Cuba).
  • The tax registration is cancelled in the registry.
  • The tax ID is a placeholder such as 12-3456789 or all the same digit.
  • Still wrong after a second attempt: the vendor was asked to fix something and resubmits with the same problem (for example a near-miss name, where a lookalike name is a known fraud tactic).
  • No reply within {{ fix_deadline_days }} days to a fix request: the submission closes by itself.
  • A reviewer rejects it.

Anything the system cannot positively verify is never approved automatically.

How the checks are sorted

Eight checks run in order: completeness, country routing, format checks, documents, identity checks, name match, verification (registry, sanctions, embargo, placeholder IDs), duplicate check. Each finding is one of three kinds:

  • Vendor can fix: a missing field or document, a typo in a format, a name that nearly matches the bank account holder, a legal form that differs (LLP vs Private Limited).
  • Needs review: a bank account already used by another vendor, a possible sanctions-list match, an onboarded vendor whose bank account or details changed, a company ID under a different name, a shared phone or email, a foreign bank account, an unsupported country, names in a non-Latin script.
  • Contradiction: the tax ID belongs to another country, GSTIN and PAN disagree, the PAN type does not fit the entity, or the bank account is in a completely different company's name. One alone is fixable; two at once reject.

If the vendor is asked to use an account in the company's own name and sends the same account again, a person reads their explanation (for example a signed letter of authorisation).

What verification uses

The registry and the watchlist are demo data written by samples.py (registry.json, sanctions.json); the names on the watchlist are fictional. They show where real services plug in: the tax authorities and the official OFAC, EU and UN lists. A tax ID the demo registry does not know is not checked, and the stage says so. For German VAT IDs the real EU VIES check can be switched on with VIES_LIVE=1; it is off by default because the sample VAT IDs are not real registrations. Whether the bank account really belongs to the vendor is not checked: that needs a bank's account-holder name service.

What happens after the verdict

  • A reviewer's decision becomes the vendor's current status; the rules' original verdict is kept next to it.
  • Request more info keeps the submission open, waiting for the vendor.
  • A corrected resubmission is linked to the earlier submission, which is then marked replaced. The new submission is also checked against what the vendor was asked to fix.
  • Every update goes to the vendor automatically, in one email thread per application (the Messages page; simulated, nothing is actually emailed). First the verdict, for example "your application is under review, we will reach back to you if anything is needed"; then the reviewer's outcome in the same thread: approved, rejected with the reason, or the list of documents we need. The reasons and document lists are worked out from what the checks found, so the reviewer types nothing, and nothing about other vendors is revealed.
  • Approved vendors join the list the duplicate check compares against. An already-onboarded company that applies again with nothing new is rejected as a duplicate; if its bank account, contact details or address changed, a reviewer verifies the change (a bank change by calling the number already on file).

Design choices

  • Fixed rules decide, not an AI. Every outcome can be explained line by line and repeated exactly.
  • Thresholds are our own design (a bank account holder name at least 80% similar to the company name, but not identical, counts as a near miss), kept in one config file. Rule version {{ rule_version }}.
  • Nothing is overwritten. Reviewer decisions are separate records next to the original verdict.
  • Bank account numbers are masked everywhere they are shown.
  • The history starts empty. Only the vendors already on file (for the duplicate check) exist before the first submission.

Known limits

  • Registry and sanctions checks use demo data, and bank-account ownership is not checked. The embargo list is simplified.
  • Documents are stored for the reviewer to open, but their contents are not read. A forged document of the right type would pass.
  • India, US and Germany only, one bank account per submission. No document expiry tracking.
  • Messages to vendors are shown on the Messages page, not emailed, and reminders are listed as due, not sent.
  • Data lives in a SQLite file; on free hosting it can reset on restart.
